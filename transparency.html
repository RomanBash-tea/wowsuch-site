1.Merge-mining LTC + DOGE

Litecoin (LTC) and Dogecoin (DOGE) both use the Scrypt hashing algorithm, and Dogecoin adopted AuxPoW (merged mining) specifically so it could share Litecoin's network security. In practice, that means a single Antminer L9 pointed at our pool hashes one proof-of-work problem that is simultaneously valid for both chains — the same GH/s, the same watts, and one hashboard, but two independent block-reward streams: an LTC reward stream and a DOGE reward stream.

Both streams are tracked separately by the pool as blocks are found on each chain, and both land together in your daily payout — you receive LTC and DOGE, not a converted or blended single coin. This is standard, publicly documented Scrypt/AuxPoW behavior, not a WowSuch-specific mechanism; you can verify the LTC↔DOGE merged-mining relationship independently (e.g. Litecoin Project / Dogecoin Foundation documentation).

2.The 3% pool fee, in context

WowSuch Pool charges a flat 3% fee, deducted from your gross mining rewards before the daily payout is calculated — you never receive an invoice for it separately, it's simply netted out. That 3% covers pool operation: running and securing the stratum/pool infrastructure that finds blocks, tracks shares, and calculates and distributes payouts. It is separate from hosting ($0.079/kWh all-in, disclosed on buy.html and terms.html §4), which is its own line item withheld independently.

We are not publishing a further breakdown of pool-fee cost allocation beyond "pool operation" — we don't want to state a specific split (e.g. infrastructure vs. margin) that we can't stand behind with real numbers. If a more granular breakdown becomes something Roman and Igor want to publish, it will be added here with real figures, not estimated ones.

3.Payout scheme

Open question — not yet published: the specific payout scheme (PPS, PPLNS, or FPPS) that WowSuch Pool runs is not documented anywhere in our public site, terms, or internal materials that we could find. Rather than guess or imply one, we're marking it as pending and routing the question to the team.

Payout scheme: to be published — ask us. In general terms across the mining industry: PPS (Pay Per Share) pays a fixed amount per share submitted regardless of whether the pool finds a block, shifting variance onto the pool; PPLNS (Pay Per Last N Shares) pays out proportionally to shares contributed in a rolling window only when blocks are actually found, shifting variance onto miners; FPPS (Full Pay Per Share) is PPS for the block subsidy plus a share of actual transaction fees. Which of these — or which variant — WowSuch Pool runs will be confirmed and published here once we have a definitive answer.

4.Profitability calculator methodology

The profit calculator on buy.html models your expected daily payout from four inputs:

  • Scrypt network difficulty — how hard it currently is to find a block on the shared LTC/DOGE Scrypt network. Higher difficulty means a fixed hashrate earns fewer coins per day. The calculator's difficulty trend chart is illustrative, not a live feed; live difficulty at calc-time is what actually determines your payout.
  • LTC and DOGE spot prices — fetched live when the calculator page loads, so the dollar-value estimate reflects current market prices, not a stale snapshot.
  • An uptime assumption — the calculator assumes the machine is hashing continuously; real-world downtime (repairs, curtailment) reduces actual output proportionally to hours offline.
  • Pool luck variance — even at constant hashrate and difficulty, actual blocks found fluctuate around the statistical expectation. The calculator shows an expected value, not a guaranteed one.

The shape of the formula, in plain language:

daily reward ≈ (your hashrate ÷ network hashrate) × block reward × 2 coins (LTC + DOGE) adjusted for: network difficulty, pool luck variance minus: 3% pool fee minus: hosting cost, if hosted ($0.079/kWh all-in) = estimated daily payout (LTC + DOGE, converted to USD at live spot prices for display)
Open question — not yet sourced: the $0.079/kWh hosting rate used throughout this site and in the calculator does not currently have a cited source (utility rate sheet, contract, or facility disclosure). We're not publishing a fabricated citation for it — it's marked here as pending until the team supplies one.

Every calculator output is an estimate, not a promise — see the risk disclosure in terms.html §8.

7.What we don't publish yet

To be direct about the boundary of this page: we do not publish real pool account identifiers, worker names, or on-chain wallet addresses anywhere on this site, including here. Disclosing those would let anyone verify pool activity independently — which is exactly what full transparency would look like — but it is a deliberate trade-off against our telemetry sanitization policy (never expose real fleet pool accounts or worker identifiers via any public endpoint), and it requires an explicit, scoped decision from Roman and Igor. It is not something this page decides on its own.

Until that decision is made, Sections 3 and 4 above list the two other specifics (payout scheme, power-rate source) still pending. All three open questions are tracked in one place internally and will be answered here, with citations, once resolved — not guessed at in the meantime.
